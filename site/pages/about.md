# About Roadbeat

Roadbeat is a free web app for people who drive, ride or take public transport in Queensland. It brings together live road conditions, traffic cameras, public transport, timetables, bushfire warnings, storm, flood and cyclone warnings, flood gauges, air quality, power outages and council and police notices, so you can check your trip in one place.

## Official data only

Every time, position, delay and count in Roadbeat comes from an official source. Roadbeat does not fill gaps with estimates. News stories are the one exception to official sources, and they are always labelled with the outlet that published them. A bus between two GPS reports is shown where it last reported. A journey that needs a walk between stops is handed to Translink's own journey planner, because no source publishes walking times. When a source has no data, Roadbeat says so.

## Where the data comes from

- **Road events, cameras, rest areas, truck restrictions and the Waze reports QLDTraffic shows:** the Department of Transport and Main Roads, through the feeds behind qldtraffic.qld.gov.au.
- **Rain radar:** the Bureau of Meteorology's radar images, as QLDTraffic publishes them.
- **Public transport:** Translink's published timetables (GTFS) and live feeds (GTFS-RT) for South East Queensland and Cairns, and the published timetables of Translink's 17 other regional networks, with live times wherever Translink's feed for that region carries them.
- **Bushfire warnings:** the Queensland Fire Department's current incidents feed.
- **Storm, flood and cyclone warnings:** Queensland's severe weather, flood and cyclone warnings feed from Queensland Disaster Management on disaster.qld.gov.au, with each warning's text, area and issuing agency as published.
- **Stream heights, rainfall, signal traffic, planned council road closures, CBD car park spaces, pedestrian counts, corridor travel times and council events:** Brisbane City Council's open data.
- **Clearways, low clearances, public toilets, accessible parking, parking meters, bike racks, bikeways, bus stop and ferry terminal access and the February 2022 flood extent:** Brisbane City Council's open data. Toilets come from the National Public Toilet Map, and rail bridge clearances from Queensland Rail.
- **Station access and facilities:** Queensland Rail's station details, published by Brisbane City Council. Lift and escalator outages come from Translink's alerts.
- **Statewide stream heights:** the latest reading at the Queensland Government's open gauging stations, from the Department of Local Government, Water and Volunteers' Water Monitoring Information Portal.
- **Flood warning areas:** the Bureau of Meteorology's National Flood Gauge Network, used to link each Brisbane gauge to BoM's flood warning for its area.
- **Crash history:** the Department of Transport and Main Roads' crash data on data.qld.gov.au.
- **Air quality:** the Department of Environment, Tourism, Science and Innovation's hourly air quality feed, and its Air Quality API for each station's history.
- **Council notices, evacuation centres, council cameras and Energex power outages:** the disaster dashboards of 58 Queensland councils. The power outages are Energex's, as the councils' dashboards relay them.
- **Police media releases:** the Queensland Police Service.
- **Earthquakes:** Geoscience Australia's earthquakes of the last seven days in or off Queensland.
- **News stories:** the headline, link and first sentence of reports in the Queensland feeds of ABC News, 7NEWS, Brisbane Times, Guardian Australia and SBS News. Each links to the publisher's own report.
- **Satellite hotspots:** Geoscience Australia's Digital Earth Australia Hotspots, which gathers heat detected by NASA, NOAA, Japanese and other satellites. A hotspot is heat a satellite saw, not a confirmed fire.
- **Satellite image:** the Japan Meteorological Agency's Himawari-9 satellite every 10 minutes, as infrared or visible from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services, or as infrared or true colour from JMA's own weather map (Source: Japan Meteorological Agency website), and the daily true-colour passes of NOAA-20 (afternoon) and Terra (morning) from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services. The map shows one at a time, labelled with the image's own time. We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by services from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS).
- **Fire bans:** the Queensland Fire Department's fire ban feed, shown in its own words.
- **Fires near the border:** the NSW Rural Fire Service's major incidents just south of the Queensland border.
- **Tropical cyclones:** the US Joint Typhoon Warning Center's warnings and forecast tracks. These are not Australia's official cyclone warnings, which come from the Bureau of Meteorology, and Roadbeat says so wherever they appear.
- **Fuel at service stations:** whether each station has fuel, from the Queensland Government's fuel price reporting as council disaster dashboards publish it. Prices aren't shown.
- **Speed and red light cameras:** the Queensland Government's map of fixed speed, red light and point-to-point camera sites. Mobile camera sites are linked, because the state publishes them by road and suburb only.
- **Past cyclone tracks:** NOAA's International Best Track Archive (IBTrACS), the positions agencies reported for cyclones near Queensland since 1980. History for context only.
- **Model weather forecast:** on each place page, raw output from Germany's DWD ICON weather model via Open-Meteo. It is a computer forecast, not an official one, and is labelled with the time the model ran.
- **Maps:** OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles and OpenStreetMap contributors.

For every feed, how often Roadbeat reads it, and whether it's answering right now, see [Feed status](/status). On a slow connection, [the text-only list](/roads) has every current road event by district and road, with no map, and keeps its last copy for when you're offline. What the warning levels and road terms mean is on the [Glossary](/glossary/).

Most of this data is published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence: that of the Department of Transport and Main Roads, Translink, the Queensland Fire Department, Brisbane City Council, the Department of Environment, Tourism, Science and Innovation, the Department of Local Government, Water and Volunteers, the Department of Resources, the Queensland Police Service, Queensland Disaster Management's warnings, Geoscience Australia, the NSW Rural Fire Service and the Bureau of Meteorology's flood gauge network. The model forecast is Open-Meteo's, under the same licence, from the DWD's open data. Roadbeat credits each publisher as that licence asks. The Bureau of Meteorology's radar images are shown as QLDTraffic publishes them. The councils' dashboards publish no licence; their notices, centres and outages are public safety information, shown as written and linked to each dashboard. NASA's imagery has no use restrictions. Himawari-9 imagery is the Japan Meteorological Agency's, under its Public Data License, which asks for the source to be named, as Roadbeat does on the map. JTWC's warnings and NOAA's IBTrACS are US Government work, credited and, for IBTrACS, cited as NOAA asks. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors under the Open Database Licence. News headlines and summaries belong to their publishers, and Roadbeat shows only what a feed reader would.

## Find a station, line or place

Three directories list what the app covers, as plain pages a search engine can read: every [train station, tram stop and ferry terminal](/stations/) in South East Queensland, every [Translink line and regional route](/lines/), and every [Queensland town, suburb and locality](/places/). Each page names only what Translink and the Queensland place names gazetteer publish, and opens the live view in the app.

## Support Roadbeat

Roadbeat is free to use, with no ads and no account. If it's useful to you, you can support it at [Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/realbakari).

## What Roadbeat is not

Roadbeat is an independent project. It is not run by, affiliated with or endorsed by the Queensland Government, Translink, the Queensland Fire Department, Brisbane City Council or any other source above. For official road information, call 13 19 40 or visit qldtraffic.qld.gov.au. For public transport, call 13 12 30 or visit translink.com.au.

> [!WARNING]
> Roadbeat is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call Triple Zero (000).

## How Roadbeat is built

Roadbeat is a web app built with React, MapLibre and Cloudflare's Kumo components. It runs in your browser and does not need an account.
